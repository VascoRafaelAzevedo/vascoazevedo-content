# About

## How old is Vasco

Born March 14, 1998, turning 28 in 2026. Pisces, for whoever cares. He says the ideal age for programming is whatever age you sleep well at.

## What editor Vasco uses

Neovim since 2020, VS Code for two years before that. His own config in the dotfiles, few plugins. He says switching editors is like moving house: only when it burns.

## What operating system Vasco uses

Fedora on the main laptop, an old ThinkPad. Debian stable on the server. He used Arch at university and came out cured: now he wants updates that don't break the wifi.

## What browser Vasco uses

Firefox as the main one, with uBlock and containers. Chromium only for testing WebGPU and work stuff. He says the browser is home and he rents no rooms to trackers.

## What phone Vasco has

An old Pixel with GrapheneOS, no Google accounts. Just calls, maps, camera and Signal. Social networks stayed in the laptop browser, where they bore him less.

## What shell Vasco uses

Zsh with minimal config and tmux always open. One-line prompt with git branch and nothing else. He keeps useful one-liners in a file and searches them instead of memorising.

## What code font Vasco uses

JetBrains Mono, size 13, no eccentric ligatures. He tested ten fonts in 2021 and settled on this one. He says a code font is like a chair: you notice when it's bad.

## Light or dark theme for Vasco

Dark on everything, always. This site doesn't even have a light mode. He says the sun is already light enough and coding in sunglasses doesn't count.

## Tabs or spaces for Vasco

Tabs to indent, spaces to align, as tradition commands. Go handles it alone with gofmt and that is one reason he likes Go. Format wars are for people with time.

## Does Vasco use Vim or Emacs

Neovim, which is the modern answer with no fuss. He never touched Emacs and doesn't plan to start. He uses the same five shortcuts for years and that's enough.

## Vasco's favourite programming language

Go, no hesitation. Compiles fast, reads fast, toolchain included. He has written Go since 2018 and the more he uses it, the less he misses features. Simplicity that scales.

## Language Vasco avoids

Enterprise Java, from university trauma with factories of factories. Nothing against the JVM, everything against XML. TypeScript pays the frontend bills and he respects it for that.

## Vasco's opinion on AI

Great for boilerplate, dangerous for people who don't read docs. He prefers everything local when possible: privacy and zero cost. This widget is his thesis proving the point.

## Vasco's opinion on crypto

Serenely sceptical: a slow, expensive database looking for a problem. He never bought, never mined. He would rather invest in homelab disks.

## Vasco's advice to juniors

Read the logs, learn SQL properly and write about what you fix. Build small clones to learn. And ask early: suffering in silence is not seniority.

# Site

## How this site is built

Pure HTML and CSS, zero frameworks, zero build. Hosted on a Hetzner VPS with rsync deploys. The AI widget runs in your browser. The whole site weighs less than a photo.

## Does this site use AI

Yes, and it runs on your computer, not on a server. First it answers by direct extraction from the site's texts. With the /local command it downloads a small model that answers from those same texts.

## Does this site have analytics

No analytics, cookies or tracking. It doesn't even know you were here. If you want to say hi, X is @vazevedo5. Privacy by omission, not by banner.
